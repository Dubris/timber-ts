    Contextualizer¶

    The contextualizer (paper module C) lets the signal content of each patch flow across the other channels and over time, into the past of its own channel. A patch on its own is a short window of one channel; after the contextualizer, it carries the state of the other channels at that moment and the history of its channel, which is what captures the spatio-temporal structure of the recording. Its output, the contextualized patches z, is the input of the encoder and of the TACO decoder.

    What it does¶

    The contextualizer factorises the attention over the two dimensions of the patch grid (channel, patch). Each of its num_layers layers (3 in the paper) runs:

    • an attention over channels at each patch time: all the channels attend to each other, at a given time;
    • a attention over time for each channel: the patches of a channel attend along time, and with is_causal=True a patch only sees the patches before it;

    followed by a feed-forward block. Factorising the two passes, instead of attending over every pair of patches, reduces the cost of the attention from O(N_c^2 N_t^2) to O(N_t N_c^2 + N_c N_t^2): with the paper's sizes (1,024 channels, 15 patches) it is about a 15-fold speed-up. The two passes use the same attention parameters by default (share_attention_weights=True) and run in the order attention_order ("channel_time": channels first, as in the paper).

    The inputs¶

    Each patch enters the contextualizer as two projections that are summed: a projection of the signal of the patch (patch_length samples to hidden_dim) and a projection of its position. The position is the channel embedding, and, when a time embedding is configured, the time embedding too (see Tokenizer). The two projections are summed rather than concatenated, so that masked patches can drop their whole signal term, bias included.

    Whether the position includes time depends on the objective:

    • for TACO, as in the paper, the position is the channel embedding only: the temporal order is inferred from the causal attention, and no time-position embedding is given;
    • for the vanilla reconstruction objective, the paper adds sinusoidal time-position embeddings, and removes the causal mask, since the time order is then given explicitly.

    Options that matter¶

    Option Default Experiments Effect
    hidden_dim 128 128 Width of the layers (D).
    num_layers 3 3 Number of channel and time layers (L_pre); 0 removes the attention.
    num_heads 4 4 Attention heads per pass.
    ffn_ratio 2 2 Widening of the feed-forward blocks.
    is_causal True True Causal attention along time: required by TACO, see Decoders.
    attention_order "channel_time" "channel_time" Which pass comes first in each layer.
    share_attention_weights True True One attention module for both passes.
    use_gradient_checkpointing False False Recompute the layers in the backward pass to save memory.

    Reference¶

    timber.modules.contextualizer ¶

    Contextualizer (paper module C): factorised spatial / temporal attention over patches.

    Each patch is embedded as signal_projection(signal) + position_projection(position) (position = channel and time embeddings concatenated), then every layer attends over the channel axis at each patch time and over the patch (time) axis at each channel, followed by a feed-forward block. The pass over time can be causal, so that a contextualised patch only depends on the past (needed for the TACO forecasting objective).
